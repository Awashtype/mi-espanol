# Mi Español: success, evidence and the free student pilot

Product direction set by Thea on October 28, 2026.

## Purpose

Help a learner independently express what they mean when a similar situation comes up again, using a learned construction or a natural equivalent. Increase their willingness to explore Spanish and their ability to have meaningful conversations with people. The tool should expand what they can do with other people and on their own.

Saved sentences are examples of a transferable ability, not scripts to reproduce verbatim. Recognizing a card, passing dictation, or reproducing a known translation is practice evidence. It is not yet proof of spontaneous transfer. App visits, streaks, deck size and time spent are operational signals, not the goal.

This applies Ivan Illich's *Tools for Conviviality* to learner autonomy and human relationships, and Donella Meadows's *Leverage Points* to the choice of system goal. Those are product-design interpretations, not claims that the essays establish a language-learning method.

- https://panarchy.org/illich/conviviality.html — excerpt from chapter II of *Tools for Conviviality*.

## The evidence we want

| Dimension | Question | Evidence | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent transfer | Can I express the intended meaning in a new situation several days later without a Spanish cue? | First baseline versus a delayed, different prompt targeting the same communicative ability; learner outcome and, where volunteered, tutor assessment | Learner ratings are self-report; a small uncontrolled pilot cannot prove causality |
| Willingness to learn/speak | Do I feel more willing to try another conversation, ask a question or explore a topic? | A short optional weekly reflection; voluntary examples of a next action | Returning to an app alone does not demonstrate motivation or learning |
| Human use | Did a relevant opportunity arise, and could I express myself with a person? | Optional after-lesson check-in or a tutor's brief observation during normal conversation | No relevant opportunity means not observed, not failed; an omitted phrase in ASR is not proof of inability |

For a defined probe set, report the number and proportion of eligible attempted objectives expressed independently, with counts and timing. Keep self-report and tutor-assessed results separate. Compare within a learner across matched, changed situations. Report missing follow-ups and attrition separately. Do not turn skipped checks into zeros or compare raw rates across learners with different opportunities.

Accept useful paraphrases and imperfect but intelligible speech. Assess whether the learner communicated the intended meaning appropriately, and whether they needed a cue. A keyword inserted unnaturally is not success. Do not penalize a natural alternative merely because it does not match a saved answer.

## A short cycle

1. Learner chooses a real communicative intention, often from a tutor lesson: ask for a file, explain a delay, tell a story, make a plan.
2. Curate a few phrases that support that intention; use one learning objective per card and preserve existing familiarity when examples change.
3. Rehearse a short conversation. Start with the most familiar targets, Essential before Useful at the same level, then gradually add shakier material and one small twist.
4. On a later day, try a fresh context without a Spanish phrase bank. This is assessment; it should not simply repeat the easiest warm-up or the saved translation.
5. If a relevant human conversation happens, the learner may add a short check-in. A tutor may spend a minute noticing one success and one gap during a normal lesson.
6. Use evidence of the remaining gap to shape the next round. Do not auto-advance card stages or change due dates from a conversation score.

## Low-pressure evidence

The pilot does not require recording everyday conversations. The learner can say, in their own words, “I wanted to explain a delay; I managed it without help,” or “I got stuck asking for another time.” A teacher's short note or a voluntarily supplied excerpt can add context. Transcript-based analysis is a later, opt-in path using an explicitly shared lesson/excerpt; absent, ambiguous or mistranscribed evidence is labelled uncertain. Nobody is required to submit a transcript, and other speakers' personal disclosures do not belong in a shared curriculum.

The app should support opportunities for human conversation. For the initial cohort, the tutor can suggest optional paired role-play or a question to try in the next lesson. A public social feed, rankings, stranger matching and always-on capture are outside the first pilot.

## First pilot proposal

A suggested scope is 5–8 volunteer students from one tutor's cohort for four weeks, at no charge from Mi Español. Participation and feedback are optional. ChatGPT sign-in is used for accounts; conversation practice opens ChatGPT and features depend on each learner's account. This does not promise unlimited third-party AI usage.

- Start: explain the purpose and data choices; each learner selects a few relevant intentions and makes a first attempt.
- Each week: short phrase practice, conversation reconstruction and an optional reflection. A tutor may independently observe a few volunteered attempts during an existing lesson.
- From the next lesson onward: use changed-context follow-ups, with at least three days between a first attempt and the corresponding follow-up in the preview.
- End: review within-learner examples of transfer, willingness and human use; ask what felt burdensome and whether students want to continue. Learn from dropouts as well as active participants.

Judge whether the tool helped learners say something they previously struggled to say. Set decision thresholds with the tutor after seeing baseline tasks; do not invent a precise efficacy percentage in advance. A positive pilot justifies another iteration, not a claim of proven fluency improvement.

## Preview and release boundary

A separate student-pilot Site has been prepared: https://mi-espanol-student-pilot.wrytoad39.chatgpt.site . Its project ID is `appgprj_6ae1f7bf0e7c81919c0380dde6864983`. The complete source is in its own Sites source repository. It remains owner-private until the audience is chosen.

The preview contains 20 neutral starter phrases, three original fictional scenes, separate per-user progress, optional changed-context self-assessment probes, and optional real-conversation/motivation check-ins. The twenty selected audio clips are the only included recordings. Feedback is stored privately by authenticated Site-scoped user ID; learners can delete their check-ins without deleting their study progress. No teacher automatically receives student data. No personal lesson archive or upstream personal deck is included, and the preview does not expose the personal transcript MCP tools.

Still needed before student release: choose invitation-only access versus a link-accessible sign-in pilot, confirm the tutor's participation and cohort, and verify an actual student sign-in and their private feedback/progress separation. No invitations have been sent. Tutor assessment and cohort analysis remain a human pilot procedure; they are not an implemented shared analytics dashboard. Current in-app check-ins are explicitly self-report. Thea's existing personal app, history and transcript archive remain separate.
